
**The minimum stack that actually works, per subject:**

1. **One primary source** — a single book or single video course that covers the full syllabus. Not the "best" one exhaustively researched for a week — any well-regarded one, picked in a day.
2. **One PYQ bank** — 8-10 years of past papers, ideally topic-sorted. This is arguably the single most important resource in the entire list.
3. **One mock test series** — full-length, exam-format tests. Doesn't need to be from multiple providers.
4. **One running notebook** — your own formula sheets, error log, and condensed notes, built as you go, not bought.
5. **One doubt-resolution channel** — a mentor, senior, forum, or even a well-used AI assistant — somewhere specific questions get answered instead of sitting unresolved.





That's it. Everything past this is optional polish, not a requirement.




**What each resource type is actually *for*:**

| Resource type | What it's for | What it's *not* for |
|---|---|---|
| **Textbook / structured course** | Building the initial concept scaffold, once, properly | Repeated revision (too slow — use your own notes instead) |
| **Past papers (PYQs)** | Telling you what to study and in what depth — your real syllabus | Learning concepts from scratch (they assume you already know the topic) |
| **Mock tests** | Training time management, stamina, and exam reflexes | Learning new content (too late-stage and inefficient for that) |
| **Self-made notes / formula sheets** | Fast, high-frequency revision in the final weeks | Your first exposure to a topic (writing notes on something you don't understand yet just copies confusion into a nicer format) |
| **Error log** | Finding your personal failure patterns across weeks | A one-time fix — it only works if reviewed on a schedule |
| **Video lectures** | Topics that don't click from text alone, or need visual/spatial intuition | Wholesale replacement for problem-solving practice |
| **Forums / doubt platforms / mentor** | Unblocking specific stuck points fast | General study strategy (too much conflicting advice, causes thrashing) |
| **Toppers' interviews / strategy posts** | Calibrating your book list and rough approach, once, early | Copying someone else's exact schedule — their constraints aren't yours |





**The test for adding any new resource:** ask "does this replace something I'm using, or does it duplicate something I already have?" If it duplicates, skip it — a second book on a topic you've already covered rarely beats spending that time on PYQs or mocks instead.
