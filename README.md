## `02 // THE FOUR-PHASE FRAMEWORK`

```text


The sequence matters.

Do not skip Phase 1 and immediately start “studying.”

Going in blind is one of the biggest time-wasters at the zero-knowledge stage.
```

⸻

## PHASE 01 — RECON
```text
WEEK 1 · BEFORE YOU STUDY A SINGLE TOPIC

Map the exam, not the subject.

You’re not learning the field yet.

You’re learning the shape of the test.

Objectives

* Pull the official syllabus PDF.
* Understand the exact marking scheme:
    * Marks per section
    * Negative marking
    * Time limits
    * Question types
* Download 8–10 years of past papers.
* Sort PYQs by topic.
* Count how frequently each topic appears.
* Identify:
    * High-frequency topics
    * High-weightage topics
    * Rare topics
    * Difficult vs. easy topics
* Find 2–3 topper strategies/interviews for the exact exam.
* Copy their resource/book list, not necessarily their timeline.

Your real syllabus is not just the official PDF.

Your real syllabus = Official syllabus + PYQ frequency + marks distribution.
```
⸻

## PHASE 02 — FOUNDATION

```text
WEEKS 2–N · ONE PASS, BUILT FOR RECALL

Learn each topic once — properly.

The goal isn’t mastery yet.

The goal is to build a scaffold that later practice can attach to.

Rules

1. One source per topic

Use:

ONE BOOK
    OR
ONE COURSE

Not:

Book A
+ Book B
+ Book C
+ YouTube Playlist
+ Notes
+ Telegram PDF

Comparing sources at this stage burns time without necessarily improving understanding.
```
⸻

## 2. Use active recall

```
After finishing a topic:

READ
 ↓
CLOSE THE SOURCE
 ↓
RECALL FROM MEMORY
 ↓
WRITE / EXPLAIN
 ↓
CHECK GAPS
 ↓
FIX GAPS

Do not immediately reread everything.
```


## PHASE 03 — DRILLING

```bash
MID-PREP · WHERE THE SCORE IS ACTUALLY BUILT

This phase should typically be 2–3× longer than foundation.

Understanding a concept and being able to solve exam questions on it are different skills.
```

## Build an Error Log

Every wrong answer should have a reason.

Error Type	Example
Concept Gap	Didn’t understand the underlying concept
Silly Mistake	Calculation / sign / arithmetic error
Time Pressure	Knew the concept but couldn’t finish
Misread	Misinterpreted the question
Formula Error	Forgot or misapplied formula
Guessing Error	Took an unjustified guess

Review schedule

Revisit errors after:

DAY 3
  ↓
DAY 10
  ↓
DAY 30

You’re applying spaced repetition to your mistakes, not just to textbook content.

⸻

## Formula Reflex

For numerical / technical exams:

Separate:

CONCEPT PRACTICE

from:

CALCULATION SPEED

Perform timed drills for:

* Formula recognition
* Substitution
* Calculation
* Approximation
* Unit conversion
* Numerical shortcuts

The goal is not just knowing the formula.

The goal is recognizing which formula to use under time pressure.

03 // WHAT TO STUDY FIRST

THE PRIORITY MATRIX

At zero knowledge, the temptation is:

Chapter 1 → Chapter 2 → Chapter 3 → ...

Don’t automatically do that.

Instead, classify topics according to:

* PYQ frequency
* Marks weightage
* Difficulty
* Time required to master

⸻

Priority Matrix

                    DIFFICULTY
                 LOW          HIGH
              ┌───────────┬───────────┐
         HIGH │           │           │
              │  STUDY    │  STUDY    │
  WEIGHTAGE   │  FIRST    │  DEEP     │
              │           │           │
              ├───────────┼───────────┤
         LOW  │           │           │
              │  SKIM /   │  LAST, IF │
              │  SKIP     │  TIME     │
              │           │  REMAINS   │
              └───────────┴───────────┘

Q1 — STUDY FIRST

High weightage + Low difficulty

Fast points.

Master these before anything else.

This is where early score gains happen.

⸻

Q2 — STUDY DEEP

High weightage + High difficulty

These deserve serious time investment.

Schedule dedicated deep-work blocks.

⸻

Q3 — SKIM / SKIP

Low weightage + Low difficulty

Read once.

Don’t spend disproportionate practice time here.

⸻

Q4 — LAST, IF TIME REMAINS

Low weightage + High difficulty

Lowest ROI category.

Attempt only after the high-value areas are solid.

⸻

Priority Rule

A topic appearing repeatedly across recent papers should receive serious attention regardless of how difficult it initially feels.

For example:

7+ appearances in the last 10 papers
                ↓
        NON-NEGOTIABLE

## 04 // THE MEMORY CURVE

The Problem

You study something today.

Then:

DAY 0  → "I know this."
DAY 3  → "I vaguely remember."
DAY 10 → "Wait... what was this?"
DAY 30 → "I have no idea."

This is normal.

The solution isn’t necessarily more hours.

It’s better-timed recall.

⸻

Spaced Recall Model

INITIAL LEARNING
      │
      ▼
   DAY 3
      │
      ▼
   DAY 10
      │
      ▼
   DAY 30
      │
      ▼
   DAY 90

Each successful recall strengthens the memory and makes subsequent forgetting slower.

Practical rule

When you learn an important topic:

LEARN
 ↓
ACTIVE RECALL
 ↓
DAY 3 REVIEW
 ↓
DAY 10 REVIEW
 ↓
DAY 30 REVIEW
 ↓
DAY 90 REVIEW

Don’t repeatedly reread. Repeatedly retrieve.

⸻

05 // OPERATING TACTICS

The techniques that make the framework actually work.

⸻

RECALL — Active Recall

Active recall > rereading

Close the book.

Then:

* Write what you remember.
* Explain the concept.
* Reproduce formulas.
* Solve without looking.

The struggle to retrieve information is part of what strengthens memory.

Rereading creates familiarity.

Retrieval creates accessibility.

⸻

## TEACH — Feynman Technique

Explain the topic to an imaginary beginner.

Rules:

NO JARGON
NO COPYING
NO LOOKING AT NOTES

When you get stuck:

STUCK POINT
    ↓
UNDERSTANDING GAP
    ↓
FIX THAT GAP

Don’t restart the entire chapter.

⸻

ELIMINATE — MCQ Elimination Ladder

For objective exams:

STEP 1
Eliminate obviously wrong options
        ↓
STEP 2
Eliminate "half-right" options
        ↓
STEP 3
Compare remaining options
        ↓
STEP 4
Select the best-supported answer

This is often better than trying to identify the correct answer immediately.

⸻

LOG — Error Log

Don’t maintain only:

Question → Correct Answer

Maintain:

Question
    ↓
My Answer
    ↓
Correct Answer
    ↓
WHY I GOT IT WRONG

Example:

Q: 127
Mistake: Sign error
Category: Silly mistake
Fix: Recheck negative terms

Review the pattern of your mistakes every week.

⸻

TIME — Question-Type Budgets

Know beforehand how long different question types should take.

Example:

Easy MCQ       → 30–60 sec
Medium MCQ     → 1–2 min
Long Numerical → 2–4 min
Hard Problem   → Mark + Move

These are examples only — use the actual timing appropriate to your exam.

Exam rule

If you exceed your budget:

MARK
 ↓
MOVE
 ↓
RETURN LATER

Don’t allow one question to consume the time of three easier questions.

⸻

INTERLEAVE — Mix Topics Later

Early preparation:

Topic A → Topic A → Topic A
Topic B → Topic B → Topic B

Later preparation:

A → C → B → D → A → B → C

This better resembles the real exam.

Why?

Because the exam doesn’t tell you:

“This is a Probability question. Use Method X.”

You must recognize the underlying pattern yourself.

⸻

06 // EXAM-DAY PROTOCOL

⸻

NIGHT BEFORE

NO NEW CONTENT
        ↓
LIGHT REVISION
        ↓
ONE-PAGE SHEETS
        ↓
SLEEP

Don’t sacrifice sleep for a last-minute cram session.

⸻

FIRST 5 MINUTES

Skim the paper before committing to questions.

Mentally classify:

EASY
MEDIUM
HARD / SKIP

⸻

ATTEMPT ORDER

Default strategy:

EASY + CERTAIN
       ↓
MEDIUM
       ↓
HARD

Don’t assume the printed order is the optimal order.

⸻

NEGATIVE MARKING

Know the exact penalty before the exam.

Use elimination where appropriate.

For guessing decisions, consider:

Probability of being correct
        ×
Marks gained
        -
Probability of being wrong
        ×
Penalty

Only guess when the expected value makes sense under your exam’s marking rules.

⸻

LAST 10 MINUTES

Stop starting new problems.

Use the final minutes to:

* Recheck marked answers
* Check calculations
* Verify question numbers
* Confirm OMR responses
* Confirm online selections
* Ensure every intended answer is actually recorded

Avoid losing marks to an administrative mistake after doing the hard work.

⸻

## ✕ Skipping PYQ Analysis

If you don’t analyze past papers, you may spend equal time on:

HIGH-YIELD TOPIC
        +
RARE TOPIC

That’s inefficient.

Use PYQs to determine where your time has the highest expected return.

⸻

## ✕ No Error Log

Without an error log:

MISTAKE
 ↓
FORGET
 ↓
REPEAT SAME MISTAKE
 ↓
MOCK
 ↓
REPEAT

With an error log:

MISTAKE
 ↓
CLASSIFY
 ↓
FIX
 ↓
REVIEW
 ↓
DON'T REPEAT

⸻

## ✕ First Full Mock in the Final Week

A final-week mock can reveal:

* Time-management problems
* Poor stamina
* Slow question selection
* Accuracy issues

But there may be no time left to fix them.

Start simulations early enough to iterate.

⸻

## ✕ Sacrificing Sleep

Don’t treat sleep as wasted preparation time.

STUDY
  +
RECOVERY
  =
LEARNING

An extra late-night study hour isn’t automatically productive if it damages next-day recall, attention, or accuracy.
