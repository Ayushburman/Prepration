
🎯 Target

GRE General Test is scored:

* Verbal: 130–170
* Quant: 130–170
* AWA: 0–6

For a highly competitive profile, a practical target is:

330+ overall

Ideally 165+ Quant + 165+ Verbal

For CS/engineering applicants, I’d particularly want 165–170 Quant.

⸻

🧠 The GRE preparation equation

Think:

Diagnostic → Vocabulary/Concepts → Question patterns → Timed practice → Full mocks → Error elimination

Not:

Memorise thousands of words → take exam.

⸻

Phase 1 — Diagnostic test

Day 1

Take a full-length GRE mock under real conditions.

Don’t study beforehand.

Record:

* Verbal score
* Quant score
* AWA
* Time remaining
* Questions guessed
* Vocabulary gaps
* Quant topics missed

This tells you whether you need a 1-month, 2-month, or 4-month preparation plan.

⸻

📚 VERBAL

GRE Verbal has three major skills:

1. Reading Comprehension

2. Text Completion

3. Sentence Equivalence

Vocabulary matters enormously, but vocabulary alone doesn’t produce 165+.

⸻

🔥 Vocabulary strategy

Don’t attempt to memorise:

10,000 words

Instead build a high-frequency GRE vocabulary base.

Learn words through:

Word → meaning → synonym → contrast → example → recall

For example:

Ephemeral

Don’t simply write:

ephemeral = short-lived

Create:

ephemeral = lasting for a very short time
opposite = permanent/enduring
“The popularity of the trend was ephemeral.”

Then test yourself later.

⸻

🧠 Spaced repetition

A useful schedule:

Day 1 → Day 3 → Day 7 → Day 14 → Day 30

Don’t reread word lists.

Recall the meaning before looking at the answer.

⸻

Sentence Equivalence

The key isn’t:

“Which word means this?”

It’s:

“Which two words create equivalent meanings in the sentence?”

You should often predict the blank before looking at the options.

Then eliminate choices.

⸻

Text Completion

Look for:

Contrast

Words like:

* although
* however
* despite
* yet
* nevertheless

Cause/effect

* therefore
* because
* consequently
* thus

Continuation

* furthermore
* similarly
* moreover

Understanding the sentence structure can sometimes compensate for incomplete vocabulary.

⸻

📖 Reading Comprehension

Don’t read every passage as if you’re studying literature.

Identify:

Main point

Author’s position

Evidence

Contrast

Purpose

Inference

Structure

Ask:

Why did the author write this paragraph?

That question is extremely useful.

⸻

🧮 QUANT

GRE Quant is not JEE Maths.

That’s good news.

You generally need strong command of:

Arithmetic

* Percentages
* Ratios
* Fractions
* Powers
* Roots
* Averages
* Rates
* Work
* Profit/loss

Algebra

* Equations
* Inequalities
* Functions
* Sequences
* Coordinate geometry

Geometry

* Triangles
* Circles
* Quadrilaterals
* Areas/volumes

Data analysis

* Statistics
* Probability
* Charts
* Tables

⸻

🚨 The GRE Quant trap

A JEE student may think:

“This is too easy.”

Then lose marks because of:

* Misreading
* Overthinking
* Arithmetic
* Spending too much time
* Not checking edge cases

GRE Quant rewards efficient reasoning, not complicated mathematics.

⸻

⚡ Learn GRE-specific tricks

For example, quantitative comparison.

Instead of calculating everything, ask:

Can I determine the relationship?

Try:

* Positive value
* Negative value
* Zero
* Fraction
* Extreme value
* Counterexample

Often you can solve the question much faster.

⸻

⏱️ Time management

Because GRE is computer-adaptive, you need to be comfortable moving on.

Don’t spend:

4 minutes on one question

while sacrificing three easy questions later.

A good rule:

If you don’t see a productive path, mark it and move.

⸻

📝 Mock strategy

Start with:

Week 1

1 diagnostic

Then:

Weeks 2–4

1 mock/week

Then:

Final 2–3 weeks

2 mocks/week

But again:

Mock analysis > mock count.

⸻

📊 Maintain an error log

Every mistake goes into one of these:

Vocab

Didn’t know word.

Logic

Misunderstood sentence.

RC

Missed author’s argument.

Quant concept

Didn’t know method.

Calculation

Arithmetic mistake.

Strategy

Spent too long.

Carelessness

Misread.

Your objective is to make each category shrink.

⸻

📅 A practical 8-week plan

Weeks 1–2

Quant

Complete fundamentals + diagnostic weaknesses.

Verbal

Begin vocabulary + Text Completion + Sentence Equivalence.

RC

Start short daily passages.

~2–3 hours/day

⸻

Weeks 3–4

Increase difficulty.

Daily:

30–45 min vocabulary

45–60 min verbal questions

45–60 min quant

20–30 min RC

⸻

Weeks 5–6

Heavy timed practice.

Start doing:

section-level timed tests

and weekly full mocks.

⸻

Weeks 7–8

Almost entirely:

Mocks + error correction + vocabulary revision + weak areas

No massive new resource collection.

⸻

🏆 For 330+

Your preparation should eventually look like:

Verbal

You can encounter an unfamiliar passage and quickly identify:

argument → evidence → author’s attitude → conclusion

Quant

You recognise:

what the question is testing + fastest route

Overall

You make very few careless mistakes.

⸻

If you’re coming from JEE

This is actually useful.

Your JEE background can give you a strong advantage in:

Quantitative reasoning + mathematical discipline + timed testing.

But don’t prepare GRE Quant like JEE.

You don’t need advanced calculus, rotation, difficult coordinate geometry, etc.

Your bigger challenge may be:

GRE vocabulary + English reading nuance.

So if your Quant is already strong, spend disproportionately more time on Verbal.

⸻

If I were preparing from scratch

I’d do:

2–3 months

Daily

60 min vocabulary

60 min Verbal

60 min Quant

30 min RC/error review

Then increase mock frequency near the exam.

And I’d use official ETS material as the benchmark, because third-party questions can differ from the real test.

⸻

The most important GRE rule

For JEE/GATE, you can spend a huge amount of time developing subject depth.

For GRE:

Don’t overprepare the mathematics.

Once Quant is around 165+, the biggest gains often come from:

Verbal → vocabulary → RC → timing → eliminating careless errors.
