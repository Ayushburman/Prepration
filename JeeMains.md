🎯 First: Set the target

Don’t prepare for “just qualifying.”

Think in terms of:

Target	Rough benchmark
90 percentile	Decent foundation
95	Good
98	Strong
99+	Serious preparation
99.5+	Very strong
99.8–99.9+	Top-rank territory

Percentile varies significantly with shift difficulty, so don’t treat these as fixed marks.

For a 99.5+ target, I’d prepare as if you’re aiming for 250+ capability, even if your eventual score is lower.

⸻

🧠 The fundamental JEE Main strategy

Your preparation should follow:

Theory → solved examples → PYQs → timed practice → mocks → error correction → revision

Not:

Lecture → lecture → lecture → lecture → syllabus completion → panic.

⸻

📚 Subject strategy

🧮 Mathematics

Maths is usually the biggest time sink.

Your objective isn’t to solve every difficult problem.

It’s:

Identify the questions that are worth your time.

Priority areas:

Algebra

* Quadratic equations
* Sequence & series
* Binomial
* Permutation & combination
* Probability
* Matrices & determinants
* Complex numbers

Calculus

* Limits
* Continuity/differentiability
* AOD
* Integration
* Differential equations

Coordinate

* Straight lines
* Circles
* Parabola
* Ellipse
* Hyperbola

Vector/3D

Very important because questions can be relatively scoring.

⸻

⚛️ Physics

Physics should become your high-accuracy subject.

Don’t study Physics like a collection of formulas.

For each chapter:

Concept → formula → graphical interpretation → standard models → PYQs

High-value areas include:

* Units & dimensions
* Kinematics
* NLM
* Work, energy & power
* Circular motion
* COM/collision
* Rotation
* Gravitation
* SHM
* Waves
* Thermodynamics
* Electrostatics
* Current electricity
* Magnetism
* EMI/AC
* Ray & wave optics
* Modern physics
* Semiconductors

⸻

🧪 Chemistry

Chemistry is where you can gain a massive advantage.

Treat the three branches differently.

Physical Chemistry

Concept + formula + numerical practice

Focus on:

* Mole concept
* Atomic structure
* Thermodynamics
* Equilibrium
* Electrochemistry
* Chemical kinetics
* Solutions
* Chemical/ionic equilibrium

⸻

Organic Chemistry

Don’t memorise reactions randomly.

Build:

GOC → mechanism → reaction families → PYQs

Master:

* GOC
* Isomerism
* Hydrocarbons
* Haloalkanes
* Alcohols/phenols/ethers
* Aldehydes/ketones/acids
* Amines
* Biomolecules/polymers etc.

⸻

Inorganic Chemistry

This is where NCERT becomes extremely important.

Read it repeatedly.

Especially:

* Periodic table
* Chemical bonding
* Coordination compounds
* p-block
* d/f-block
* Metallurgy-related portions where applicable

For JEE Main, don’t replace NCERT with 500-page notes.

⸻

🔥 The biggest difference from GATE

For GATE:

Depth > speed

For JEE Main:

Depth + speed + selection + accuracy

You need to develop automaticity.

For example, if you see:

\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}dx

you shouldn’t spend 30 seconds thinking about what to do.

Your brain should immediately recognise:

\ln|f(x)|+C

Same for:

* standard Physics models
* Chemistry reactions
* common mathematical transformations

Phase 2 — Syllabus completion

November–December

Finish remaining major chapters.

Start:

chapter tests + mixed tests

Don’t wait until the syllabus is 100% complete.

⸻

Phase 3 — PYQ domination

December–January

Now shift heavily toward:

JEE Main PYQs

Ideally solve multiple years of PYQs, especially recent papers.

Don’t merely check answers.

For every question ask:

Why did this method work?

⸻

Phase 4 — Mock phase

January → April

Your preparation becomes increasingly test-driven.

Start with:

1 full mock/week

Then:

2/week

Eventually:

2–3/week, depending on how well you analyse them.

⸻

📊 Your mock-analysis system

Suppose you score:

185

Don’t simply say:

“I need 65 more marks.”

Break the paper down.

Example:

Problem	Marks
Didn’t know concept	-24
Knew but couldn’t solve	-16
Calculation mistakes	-12
Silly mistakes	-8
Time management	-5

Now you know exactly where your next 65 marks can come from.

⸻

🚨 Your error notebook is extremely important

Divide it into:

Concept errors

“I didn’t know this.”

Application errors

“I knew the concept but couldn’t apply it.”

Calculation errors

“Arithmetic/algebra mistake.”

Reading errors

“I didn’t read the question correctly.”

Selection errors

“I wasted 7 minutes on a 4-mark problem.”

This is much more useful than simply writing hundreds of formulas.

⸻

⏱️ Daily schedule

If you’re aiming for 99.5+, eventually build toward:

8–10 effective hours/day

Example:

3 h — Mathematics

2.5 h — Physics

2.5 h — Chemistry

1 h — PYQ/error revision

But don’t force 10 hours immediately.

Start with whatever you can sustain and increase gradually.

8 genuinely focused hours > 12 hours of distracted studying.

⸻

🔁 The revision cycle

This is crucial.

Don’t study a chapter once.

Use:

Revision 1

After 1–2 days

Revision 2

After 7 days

Revision 3

After 21–30 days

Revision 4

Before the exam

This prevents the:

“I studied this chapter two months ago and now I remember nothing.”

problem.

⸻

🧠 The 70/30 rule

A good preparation split eventually becomes approximately:

30% learning

70% solving/revision/testing

Early preparation can be closer to 50/50.

But if you’re still spending 80% of your time watching lectures three months into preparation, that’s a warning sign.

⸻

📖 Don’t collect resources

For each subject:

One main theory source

NCERT for Chemistry

One PYQ source

One test series

Your error notebook

Enough.

The resource addiction looks like preparation but isn’t.

Near the end, you should be able to take a full paper and think:

“I know this.”

“I recognise this model.”

Maths

“This is either straightforward or not worth my time.”

⸻

⚡ Exam strategy

Don’t decide beforehand:

“I’ll do Physics → Chemistry → Maths.”

Test this during mocks.

You might discover your optimal sequence is:

Chemistry → Physics → Maths

or

Physics → Chemistry → Maths

The best order is the one that maximises your score and keeps you calm.

⸻

First pass

Solve:

🟢 Immediate questions

Don’t spend 4 minutes proving something you can revisit.

⸻

Second pass

🟡 Moderate questions

⸻

Third pass

🔴 Time-consuming questions

And maintain strict time awareness.

⸻

One huge JEE Main principle

Don’t chase difficulty. Chase marks.

If a chapter has:

80% questions that are moderate and scoring

master those before spending hours on:

5% ultra-difficult questions.

JEE Main isn’t asking you to prove you’re a mathematical genius.

It’s asking:

Can you score more than the other candidates in your shift?
